# Appendix B – Formal Requirements for Diploma Theses

English translation of [Zalacznik_B_do_Uchwaly.pdf](Zalacznik_B_do_Uchwaly.pdf)
(Faculty of Economic Sciences, University of Warsaw — WNE UW).
Original Polish text (parsed): [formal_requirements_pl.md](formal_requirements_pl.md).

Translated for reference; the Polish original is the authoritative source in
case of any discrepancy.

## General recommendations

For both master's and bachelor's theses, the author must demonstrate command
of workshop skills (documentation, presentation) and editorial skills. The
following are assessed in this respect: the construction (structure) of the
thesis, title, abstract, introduction, summary (conclusion, findings),
bibliography, footnotes, tables, figures, appendices, clarity of argument, and
language. The thesis should be written concisely. The recommended length may
be exceeded, but only in clearly justified cases. The editing of the thesis
should conform to the standards specified below, as established by WNE UW.

## Detailed notes

1. Required structure of the thesis (when written in the traditional form),
   as specified in Appendix D:
   a. title page;
   b. page with the signed declarations of the supervisor and the author(s)
      of the thesis; in the electronic version of the thesis, the page with
      the signatures should be scanned (150 dpi) in color and pasted in;
   c. page with the thesis abstract, Erasmus programme codes, and thematic
      classification;
   d. table of contents;
   e. main text;
   f. bibliography;
   g. list of lists, including:
      i. list of abbreviations (optional — if any exist),
      ii. list of tables (optional — if any exist),
      iii. list of figures (optional — if any exist),
      iv. list of appendices (optional — if any exist),
      v. appendices (optional — if any exist).

2. Font type and size for the main body text: Times New Roman or similar,
   12 pt.

3. The table of contents should include all chapter and subchapter titles.
   Up to three levels of subchapters are permitted.

4. Main text — line spacing: 1.5 lines. Single-line spacing is used for:
   titles, and captions of figures, tables, and sources — for longer titles
   and content that wraps onto the next line. Single-line spacing is also
   permitted when creating enumerated lists. Each paragraph (created with the
   Enter key) should begin with a tab — position "1" (centimeter) on the top
   ruler bar of the Word editor.

5. Margins — all four margins set to 25 mm; do not leave extra space for
   binding.

6. Page numbering — starting from the title page (numbered 0, with the number
   not displayed on that page), at the bottom of the page, centered, in Times
   New Roman or similar font, 12 pt. — applies to all sheets of the thesis.

7. Emphasis in the text. Foreign terms should be italicized, except for
   surnames and commonly used expressions. Underlined words or fragments
   should be emphasized using letter-spaced or bold text; the former is
   recommended. Quotations should be in quotation marks; longer quotations
   may be set as a block and indented relative to the main text, in which
   case single-line spacing and a smaller font (10 pt) are permitted.

8. Footnotes — in 10 pt font, at the bottom of the page, numbered
   consecutively throughout the whole thesis, justified between the margins,
   with text wrapping to a second line indented so the footnote number
   remains visible. In the main text, the place to which a footnote refers is
   marked with a sequential number written as a superscript. If a footnote
   refers to a term (word), it is placed immediately after it; if it refers
   to a sentence or several sentences (e.g., a quotation), it is placed at
   the end of the last sentence (quotation). As a rule, footnotes do not
   contain bibliographic references — they supplement points raised in the
   text.

9. Main chapters of the thesis should begin on a new page. Chapter and
   subchapter titles — bold. Chapter titles start from the left margin, with
   longer text (exceeding one line) wrapped, or are centered. Subchapter
   titles start from the left margin. Spacing within titles and subtitles:
   1 line. Spacing between chapter/subchapter titles and the text: 1.5 lines.

10. Tables — numbered from 1 to the number of the last table in the thesis,
    provided with a title and a reference to the source. The title starts at
    the left margin, followed by the number, a period, and the table title
    text; spacing from the table: one line. Longer table title text wrapping
    to further lines should be indented so that the words "Table [number]."
    remain visible. The table is centered between the side margins of the
    page. The table's header row is bold and centered within the cells.
    Table borders are single-line. Do not use shaded cell fills or colored
    fonts. In the "Item" ("Wyszczególnienie") column — where justified —
    content should be left-aligned. Where justified, the table format should
    be adjusted to the width of the page text, or placed on a separate
    landscape page. Font size should be adapted to the nature of the table;
    a 12 pt font size is recommended.

11. Figures (chart, diagram, view) — numbered, provided with a caption. The
    caption is placed below or above the figure, starting at the left margin,
    together with a sequential number. A longer figure caption exceeding one
    line should be justified and wrapped with indentation so that words such
    as "Fig. 12." remain visible. Figures are centered between the margins.
    The figure source citation uses 10 pt font. Spacing between the text
    describing the figure and the source: one line.

12. Graphics — to limit the size of the thesis file (when there are many
    graphics, or graphics with large file sizes that increase the thesis file
    size to 10–30 MB), it is recommended to prepare graphics as separate
    files in JPG or PNG format, e.g., linked into the text rather than
    embedded. The thesis file and the graphics should be located in the same
    directory (folder). Graphics should not contain substantial fragments of
    text.

13. In-text literature references — after the cited information or text,
    give the author's (authors') surname(s) and year in parentheses, e.g.,
    (Kowalski, 2015) or (Nowak et al., 2020). Do not give full bibliographic
    references in footnotes.

14. Bibliography — literature entries listed alphabetically by authors'
    surnames, institutional author names, or titles; each entry starts at the
    left margin, with text longer than one line wrapped and indented to the
    width of a tab, justified; line spacing within one entry: single; a blank
    line with single spacing between entries; preferably in APA format,
    available via Google Scholar. If the nature of the thesis requires it,
    the bibliography may be divided into sections. The description of
    materials obtained from the Internet should include information about
    the author (individual or institutional) of the document, the document
    title, if possible its creation date, the web address, and the date the
    page was accessed. Bibliography entries should not be numbered.

15. List of lists — including: list of abbreviations, list of tables, list
    of figures, list of appendices — as they appear in the text of the
    thesis; longer descriptions should be wrapped with indentation so that
    words such as "Table 12." or "Fig. 13." remain visible. Appendices should
    be treated as subtitles. The entire LIST OF LISTS section should be
    treated similarly to the TABLE OF CONTENTS.

16. Theses written in English — the structure of the thesis is the same as
    for theses in Polish. In addition, keywords must also be given in Polish
    (centered, in parentheses below the English keywords); on the abstract
    page, "Title of the thesis in English" should be changed to "Title of
    the thesis in Polish," with the Polish translation of the title entered
    below it.

17. Theses in the form of a scientific article — the structure of a thesis
    written as a scientific article is as follows:
    a. title page;
    b. page with the signed declarations of the supervisor and the author(s)
       of the thesis; in the electronic version of the thesis, the page with
       the signatures should be scanned (150 dpi) in color and pasted in;
    c. page with the thesis abstract, Erasmus programme codes, and thematic
       classification;
    d. table of contents;
    e. a page with the heading "Scientific Article" ("Artykuł naukowy");
    f. the content of the scientific article, formatted uniformly according
       to the requirements of the chosen journal; the scientific article
       includes a bibliography and any appendices;
    g. a page with the heading "Supplement" ("Suplement");
    h. the content of the supplement; the supplement may include, at the
       end, a bibliography relating to the content of the supplement;
    i. list of lists, including:
       i. list of abbreviations (optional — if any exist),
       ii. list of tables (optional — if any exist),
       iii. list of figures (optional — if any exist),
       iv. list of appendices (optional — if any exist),
       v. appendices (optional — if any exist).
