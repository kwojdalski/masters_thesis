3. Replace the six-instrument cross-sectional average in Table 4 with per-instrument statistics plus

4. Reconcile Section 5.3.3 with Figure 6 and state clearly which statistics transform the validation and

The bibliography, 71 verified entries with no phantom citations, covers the microstructure canon and           a) 4
the RL lineage, though three attributions need correcting: the signed-trade-flow feature is credited to
"Lee & Ready (1991)" [p.136] without applying that algorithm, the feature named hft_vpin is not VPIN           b) 5

checks.

6. RESULTS: Has the aim of the thesis been achieved? Have the research hypotheses been
verified? Have research questions been answered? Does the thesis have any mistakes which cast on
the correctness of driven conclusions?
The headline result, a 304.94% return with Sharpe 5.035 under frictionless mid-price execution (Table           4
4, p.85), is the thesis's central empirical claim and deserves a reconciliation pass before it is read at
face value. Three figures need recomputing or explaining: the Win plus Lose sums differ across
policies (TWAP/VWAP 20.27%, TD3 31.87%, DDPG 41.08%, PPO 48.01%) although at zero fee the
share of non-zero-return steps should not depend on the policy given that "Roughly 80-90% of
consecutive LOB returns are zero" [p.89]; the "21,776 position changes" [p.99] does not reconcile
with the 0.53 turnover printed in the same sentence over 3,673,439 events, a gap of roughly a factor
of 45; and Figure 4 shows TD3 ending with cumulative reward below zero while ranking near the top
on portfolio value in Figure 3 on the same page, which the text does not yet interpret. Extending the
bid/ask repricing [p.105] from one instrument into a six-instrument table, using data already in Table
13, would settle whether the edge survives realistic execution.

Suggested defense questions:
1. Given that the mid-price is unchanged on 80 to 90 percent of steps at zero fee, could you walk us
through how the differing Win+Lose sums across TD3 (31.87%), DDPG (41.08%), PPO (48.01%) and
the identical TWAP/VWAP (20.27%) in Table 4 arise?
2. Could you reconcile the "21,776 position changes" figure on page 99 with the 0.53 turnover printed
in the same sentence, given that 0.53 times 3,673,439 test steps implies at least roughly 970,000
non-zero position changes?
3. Would it be possible to extend the bid/ask-repriced result from one instrument to all six, using the
half-spread and per-step-edge data already in Table 13?

7. TERMINOLOGY: Was the thesis written correctly in terms of terminology? Do any terminological
errors in the thesis affect the correctness of the conclusions?
Several foundational terms need a precise redefinition, which matters because the contribution rests               1
on exact microstructure and RL terminology. The Markov property is defined twice as a property of
the optimal action rather than of the transition and reward distribution [p.31, p.47]; Kyle's lambda is
printed with the single-auction formula while attributed to the continuous-time model [p.15, p.16]; and
the feature labelled VPIN is implemented without volume bucketing or averaging, so it should either
match the definition in the glossary [p.124] or be renamed. Notation would also benefit from
standardisation: the symbol phi denotes the actor network for TD3 and DDPG but the value network
for PPO [p.62], the object priced by the simulator carries four different names [p.9, p.53, p.70, p.77],
and Algorithm 1 states "Load L3 order book dataset" [p.79] for data identified elsewhere as MBP-10.
