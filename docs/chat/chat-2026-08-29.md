## What this benchmark is meant to be

VWAP is one of the baselines TD3 gets compared against in H1. It represents a simple, mechanical trading rule: *start with no position, build up to a full position over the evaluation window, and buy more heavily during the moments when the market is busy.* "Busy" means high trading volume.

The idea is reasonable: if you have to buy over a period, buy when there's lots of volume around.

## What the code actually does

Say the evaluation window is 3 ticks, and the volume traded at each tick is:

| tick | volume |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 1 |
| 3 | 98 |

Total volume = 100. The code divides each tick's volume by that total to get the buying schedule: buy 1% of the position at tick 1, 1% at tick 2, 98% at tick 3.

So the benchmark walks into tick 2 holding 1% of a position, and into tick 3 holding 2%.

## Why that's cheating

Look at when it needs the number 100.

At tick 1, the benchmark has to decide "buy 1%." To know that 1 share is 1%, it must know the total will be 100. But at tick 1, only 1 share has traded. The 98 shares arrive at tick 3 — in the future.

So the schedule is built from volume that hasn't happened yet.

Why this changes the result: suppose the price rises between tick 1 and tick 2, then falls. The benchmark is holding almost nothing (1%) during the rise, because it "knew" to wait for the big volume later. A real trader standing at tick 1 has no idea the volume is coming and would have bought a normal-sized chunk.

In my earlier check, this was a 50× difference in the benchmark's return on identical prices. TD3 is being compared against a baseline that can see ahead.

**TWAP, the other execution benchmark, is fine** — it just buys 1/n each tick, and n is the window length, known from the start. No future information.

## Why I can't just fix it

The obvious repair is "only use volume seen so far." That doesn't work. At tick 1 you'd compute 1 ÷ 1 = 100%, at tick 2, 2 ÷ 2 = 100%. You always get 100%, because the numerator and denominator are the same number.

You need a denominator that is an *estimate of the whole window's volume*, formed in advance. And any estimate built purely from the past collapses back into "spread it evenly over time" — which is just TWAP again.

This is why real VWAP trading algorithms use historical data: they know from past days that volume is typically heavy at the open, quiet at midday, heavy at the close, and they schedule against that expected shape. The estimate comes from *outside* the day being traded.
